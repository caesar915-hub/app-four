Check-in “Listening…” — redesign

The active voice check-in screen, shown while the mic is capturing someone speaking about their state. Tapping stop is done — there's no review step, so this single screen has to feel calm, hold the moment, and stay quietly out of the way. This page proposes a final direction and shows the alternatives it was chosen over.

The three fixes

  • Waveform removed. The old animated 15-bar equalizer is gone for good. The “something is happening” signal is now a calm living cue: the How We Feel crescent slowly rotating + breathing behind the prompt, plus a single soft pulsing record dot. No bars, ever.
  • Nudges & lettering re-set with real hierarchy. Instead of three cramped cards stuffed inside the ring, the nudge is promoted to the emotional core of the screen — one gentle question, beautifully set in the serif, with a quiet supporting hint and generous breathing room.
  • Crescent freed from clutter. No more timer + waveform + stop + cancel jammed inside the ring. The crescent becomes the calm anchor/backdrop; the timer and controls have their own clean zones. Motion freezes under prefers-reduced-motion.
Recommended direction — “Single prompt, large” · refined

One nudge at a time, set large in New York as a teleprompter-calm hero, floating over the slowly breathing How We Feel crescent. This was the consensus winner of the design review — the most emotionally focused and the best match for an intimate, one-thing-at-a-time speaking moment. It is shown here refined: the judges' best grafts folded in — a confident serif “Listening…” header voice, a more legible tabular timer, a single warm serif hint beneath the prompt, reassuring stop micro-copy, a flat (no red-glow) stop button, the floating Liquid Glass tab bar, and a whisper-quiet ambient halo. The prompt cross-fades live every ~10s so you can see the rotation and the progress dots advance.

What changed vs. the current screen

Before → after

  • Animated 15-bar waveform inside the ring → removed entirely. Calm cue = breathing/rotating crescent + soft pulsing record dot.
  • Three cramped nudge cards + tiny uppercase label, stacked above the ring → one gentle serif question as the hero, with a quiet supporting hint and a “You might mention” kicker.
  • Timer + waveform + stop + Cancel all crammed inside the crescent → crescent is a clean ambient backdrop; timer moves to a refined header row; stop + Cancel get a separated control zone.
  • Plain “Listening…” headline competing with the nudge cards → serif “Listening…” demoted to a confident header voice beside the RECORDING dot and tabular timer.
  • Bare red stop square with a red-glow shadow → flat #FF453A stop (no glow) with reassuring “tap stop when you're done” copy, so stop feels calm, not alarming.
  • Kept: the exact How We Feel crescent SVG, the native palette tokens, and prefers-reduced-motion freezing all motion.
Decision needed — one prompt at a time, or keep the trio?

Single vs. trio

The hero (single, recommended) surfaces one nudge at a time and cross-fades to the next every ~10s. It is calmer, more intimate, and gives each question its own breath — the right feel for someone speaking about their mental state. But it changes app behaviour and the view model.

Today the VM exposes currentNudges: [String] — a static trio derived from nudgeTrio(forElapsed:) in CheckInViewModel.swift, and CheckInNudges renders three cards. The single direction needs the VM to expose a single currentNudge that advances on a ~10s timer (flatten the two trios into one ordered list of six prompts), and CheckInNudges to render and cross-fade one prompt. It also wants a one-line hint per nudge (grafted from v4's hint library) — a small content/model addition.

If the team prefers zero behaviour change, ship v2 (Trio, re-spaced) — it keeps the current static trio and the existing currentNudges API, and still fixes the cramped layout by re-setting the three as airy serif rows. The recommendation is the single direction; v2 is the safe fallback. Pacing caveat: a 10s auto-advance can feel like pressure if it rotates away before someone finishes a thought — tune the interval (and consider pausing rotation while voice energy is high) in the real VM.
