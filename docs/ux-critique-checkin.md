# Check-in — UX/UI Critique & Brainstorm Brief

> **Source:** opus max-effort code review (six lenses) **+ device screenshots**. Nielsen heuristics **31/40**.

> **Status:** uncommitted working doc — feeds superpowers `brainstorming` → (if a feature emerges) Spec Kit.


## 1. What it is

The Check-in tab — the app's primary capture surface and one of four tabs (DESIGN.md#L72). It is a single-screen state machine over `RecordingState` with three rendered states (CheckInView.swift#L66-79): (1) Idle "hub" — uppercase date, a Fraunces "Ready when\nyou are." headline, a breathing CrescentRing, a gradient "Speak check-in" primary, and two ghost secondaries "Log meds" / "Type note"; (2) Recording stage — a top countdown progress bar, a rotating Fraunces nudge prompt + hint + prompt dots, a spinning CrescentRing wrapping a mono timer, "Stop & save", and a "Cancel" text button; (3) Saved — a gradient checkmark that springs in, "Captured.", a calm subtitle, and "Done" / "Check in again". A `MedicationBarView` rides at the top via ScreenContainer (ScreenContainer.swift#L75). "Type note" opens TextCheckInComposer — a signals-first sheet with three GlyphRampPicker rows (mood/energy/focus), a free-text note box, and a gradient "Save check-in" pill, writing a `CheckInDraft`. "Log meds" opens MedicationLogSheet. Transcription/extraction run silently in the background after save (CheckInViewModel.swift#L109-114); no transcribing UI is shown, matching the locked decision (DESIGN.md#L108).


## 2. Verdict

This is a genuinely strong, on-brand screen — NOT AI slop. It demonstrably internalizes the product posture: no streaks, no confetti, no transcribing spinner on save, copy that grants permission ("Ready when you are.", "Captured. Talk to you next time."), and a single clear primary per state. The state machine is clean and the background-transcription-without-blocking-UI architecture (CheckInViewModel.swift#L99-120) is sophisticated and correct. Reduce Motion is honored in four distinct places. This is the work of someone who read their own DESIGN.md. The problems are concentrated in three areas. (1) Correctness: `startRecording()` has no guard against the recording-already-in-progress state (CheckInViewModel.swift#L46-85), and the auto-start path can fire it re-entrantly (CheckInView.swift#L54-59) — a real double-start bug. (2) Accessibility for the exact audience: the rotating prompts that ARE the recording UX are entirely invisible to VoiceOver (promptDots and progress bar are `accessibilityHidden`, and prompt rotation posts no announcement — CheckInView.swift#L205,L245), the TextEditor has no label (TextCheckInComposer.swift#L78), and there is no live region for "Saving…"→"Captured." (3) Sensory reward: a `Haptics` enum with `.success()` exists and is called nowhere in the entire capture flow — the one moment the whole product is built around ("I said it and it's captured") lands with zero haptic confirmation. Heuristic score 31/40: deductions cluster in error recovery, a11y-as-visibility, and the silent failure paths where a dropped recording resets to idle with no user-facing message (CheckInViewModel.swift#L116-118). Fix the re-entrancy guard, add the save haptic, and expose the recording prompts to VoiceOver, and this is a 36+.


## 3. Strengths (preserve)

- Product posture is faithfully executed: no streaks, no confetti, no transcribing UI on the saved screen (CheckInView.swift#L277, DESIGN.md#L108). The Saved state is a pure 'it's captured' confirmation — exactly the stated reward model.
- Reduce Motion is honored in four independent places, not just one: the state crossfade (CheckInView.swift#L24), the prompt slide/fade animation (L230), the countdown bar (L198), the saved checkmark pop (L317), and inside CrescentRing for both breathe and spin (CrescentRing.swift#L38,L45). This is above-average a11y discipline.
- Copy is emotionally tuned to the audience and judgment-free: 'Ready when you are.', hints like 'Heavy, light, flat, bright — whatever fits.' (CheckInViewModel.swift#L234), 'Captured. Talk to you next time.' No raw enum values leak — the SignalScaleRow readout renders 'N · Name' via displayLabel, not rawValue (TextCheckInComposer.swift#L129).
- The background-transcription architecture is correct and careful: state flips to .done immediately so the user is never blocked, transcription is serialized on the single Whisper actor across back-to-back check-ins, guarded against mid-stream deletion, and raced against a 90s timeout (CheckInViewModel.swift#L99-195). Cancelled/timed-out/failed all persist a recoverable status with retry copy.
- idleTimerDisabled is correctly toggled on at record start and off at every exit (stop, cancel, reset) — CheckInViewModel.swift#L65,L91,L200,L211 — so the screen won't sleep mid-recording and won't leak the wake-lock.
- Token discipline is high in the hub and composer: Spacing/Typography/Theme/Radius used throughout, fonts carry relativeTo: for Dynamic Type (Typography.swift), and the meadow gradient is reserved for primary/crescent/active per the rule (DESIGN.md#L42).

## 5. Screenshot layer (visual findings)

- **Confirms AI-slop PASS:** "Ready when you are." + the green→amber **CrescentRing** is genuinely beautiful and on-brand; clear primary (gradient Speak) vs secondary (Log meds / Type note).
- **Crescent earns-its-space question:** the ring occupies ~40% of the screen and (idle) is purely ambient — a newcomer may read it as a progress indicator or a tap target. Brainstorm thread: give it a job (breathing affordance / last-check-in echo) or shrink it.
- **FAB overlap:** the feedback (!) bubble overlaps the right edge of the Speak button.

## 6. Accessibility audit

- **P1** Rotating recording prompts are invisible to VoiceOver — the core recording UX has no screen-reader equivalent — The promptProgressBar is accessibilityHidden(true) (L205) and promptDots is accessibilityHidden(true) (L245). The heroPrompt collapses to a single static label of the CURRENT prompt (L231-232), but prompt rotation (every promptInterval seconds, CheckInViewModel.swift#L244-250) posts no UIAccessibility announcement. A VoiceOver user who starts recording hears the first prompt once, then nothing — they never learn the prompt advanced to 'What's your energy like?' etc. The entire guided-nudge mechanic, which is the screen's reason for existing while recording, is silent for them. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L190-246)`
- **P1** No live-region announcement for state transitions (Saving… / Captured.) — State flips idle→recording→processing(headline 'Saving…' per comment L70)→done ('Captured.') drive purely visual changes. There is no .accessibilityAddTraits(.updatesFrequently) live region or UIAccessibility.post(.announcement) on these transitions. A VoiceOver user who taps 'Stop & save' gets no spoken confirmation that the check-in was captured — the one moment that most needs an auditory 'done'. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L65-79, L298)`
- **P2** TextEditor has no accessibilityLabel; placeholder is not exposed to VoiceOver — The note placeholder Text 'Anything you want to remember about today?' is .allowsHitTesting(false) (L76) and sits behind the TextEditor in a ZStack; it is not wired as the editor's accessibility label. The TextEditor itself (L78) gets no .accessibilityLabel. A VoiceOver user focusing the field hears a generic 'text field, edit text' with no indication of what to enter. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/TextCheckInComposer.swift#L68-87)`
- **P2** Touch targets below 44pt: composer close button (30×30), glyph picker (~38pt), Cancel text button — The composer close 'xmark' is framed 30×30 (TextCheckInComposer.swift#L36) — below the 44pt minimum (Metrics.minTapTarget=44, Metrics.swift#L10). The glyph picker buttons are size 30 + Spacing.xs(4) padding ≈ 38pt (GlyphRampPicker.swift#L11,L19) — also under 44. The recording-stage 'Cancel' is a bare text Button (CheckInView.swift#L177) with only font-height tap area. The primary/secondary ButtonStyles correctly enforce 44 (Buttons.swift#L11), but these hand-rolled controls don't. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/TextCheckInComposer.swift#L33-39; GlyphRampPicker.swift#L18-19; CheckInView.swift#L177-180)`
- **P2** Crescent timer + record state not grouped/labeled as a status for VoiceOver — The recording ZStack holds a decorative spinning ring (accessibilityHidden via CrescentRing.swift#L50), a mono timer Text (CheckInView.swift#L173) that reads as a bare duration, then Stop and Cancel buttons. The timer carries no context label ('Recording, 0:42 elapsed') and isn't an .updatesFrequently live region, so a VoiceOver user neither knows they're recording nor hears elapsed time progress. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L169-182)`
- **P3** Hub headline forced line break may read awkwardly to VoiceOver and fights Dynamic Type — 'Ready when\nyou are.' hard-wraps with \n. VoiceOver typically ignores the break (reads fine), but the hard wrap prevents natural reflow at large Dynamic Type sizes and on narrow widths. Low risk; flag for the Dynamic Type pass. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L90)`
- **P3** Decorative-glyph and accessibilityHidden usage is otherwise correct — positive note — GlyphRampPicker passes decorative:true to SignalGlyph and supplies its own composed label '<title> <displayLabel>' plus .isSelected trait (GlyphRampPicker.swift#L26-27) — correct VoiceOver modeling of a selectable level. CrescentRing is correctly accessibilityHidden. SignalGlyph's signalAccessibilityLabel builds 'Energy: Alert, 4 of 5' (GlyphSignal.swift#L72). This part of the a11y model is well done. `(app-four/Views/Components/GlyphRampPicker.swift#L18-27; CrescentRing.swift#L50)`

## 7. Logic / correctness

- **P1** startRecording() is not idempotent and lacks a current-state guard — No `guard state == .idle/.done` before starting. elapsedTime is reset to 0 at L65 regardless of current state. Combined with the auto-start path (CheckInView.swift#L54, which only handles .done) and a non-disabled speakButton during the async permission round-trip, this allows a second start to zero a live timer and request a duplicate audio session. See P1 finding for the full trace. `(app-four/ViewModels/CheckInViewModel.swift#L46-85)`
- **P2** loadPromptInterval() does a synchronous SwiftData fetch on the main actor during start — On every startRecording, Self.loadPromptInterval() runs a synchronous FetchDescriptor<AppSettings> against AppModelContainer.container.mainContext (L218-220) on the @MainActor. It's a tiny single-row fetch so practically cheap, but it's a synchronous disk-touch on the hot path of the most latency-sensitive action, and it reaches into a global container rather than the injected store — a testability/coupling smell. try? silently swallows fetch errors and falls back to .relaxed. `(app-four/ViewModels/CheckInViewModel.swift#L217-224, called at L67)`
- **P2** transcribeInBackground catches CancellationError but spawns an unstructured detached finalizer that can itself be lost — On cancellation the code spawns `Task { @MainActor [store] in recording.status = .failed; ... }` to persist a recoverable status because the enclosing task is cancelled. This is a deliberate, documented workaround (comment L138-143) and is reasonable, but it is fire-and-forget: if the app is torn down in that window the status write is lost, and it mutates a @Model (recording) captured by reference outside any guarded lifetime check (unlike the streaming consumer which guards store.recordings.contains, L175). Edge case, low probability, but it's an unverified write. `(app-four/ViewModels/CheckInViewModel.swift#L137-148)`
- **P3** currentPromptIndex / promptProgress depend on a 0.1s-accumulated Double (float drift) — elapsedTime += 0.1 each tick (L281). currentPromptIndex adds a 1e-6 epsilon to counter drift at the boundary (L246-247) — a correct, self-aware fix — but accumulating 0.1 floats over an 8-minute max (480s = 4800 ticks) still accrues error, and promptProgress (L253) uses truncatingRemainder on the same drifting value with no epsilon, so the bar can momentarily read ~0.999 then snap. Minor visual only; the .id(currentPromptIndex) reset (L201) masks most of it. `(app-four/ViewModels/CheckInViewModel.swift#L244-255, L277-288)`
- **P3** levelTask consumes the audio-level stream but discards every value — startLevelMonitoring() iterates audioService.audioLevelStream and does `_ = level` (L294) — it drains the stream to nowhere. DESIGN.md#L80 specifies the recording crescent should have 'a voice-level glow.' The plumbing is subscribed but the level never reaches the UI, so the spec'd voice-reactive glow is unimplemented dead-ish wiring. Either wire it to the ring or remove the task. `(app-four/ViewModels/CheckInViewModel.swift#L290-297)`
- **P3** reset() and the autoStart consumer both write shouldAutoStart/state without a single source of truth — shouldAutoStart is consumed on both .onAppear and .onChange (L27-28); consumeAutoStart flips the binding false then starts. If onAppear and onChange fire in close succession (tab switch while binding flips), the guard `guard shouldAutoStart` (L55) makes the second a no-op — so it's safe — but the dual trigger plus the .done-only reset (L57) is fragile; one added state would reintroduce the double-start. Worth a single consolidated entry point. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L27-28, L54-59)`

## 8. Motion / transitions

- **State crossfade is Reduce-Motion-gated but uses a raw .easeInOut literal instead of a Motion token** — .animation(reduceMotion ? nil : .easeInOut(duration: 0.25), value: viewModel.state) correctly nils under Reduce Motion. But 0.25s easeInOut is hand-rolled; DESIGN.md#L83 prescribes medium 250–400ms and a Motion token (Motion.smooth = .smooth(0.4)) exists (Motion.swift#L11). The whole-screen state change arguably wants Motion.smooth, not a bespoke 0.25 curve. Consistency gap, not a bug. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L24)`
- **Hero prompt transition: animation is Reduce-Motion-gated, but the asymmetric slide .transition itself is not** — heroPrompt applies an asymmetric move+opacity .transition (L215) driven by .id(currentPromptIndex). The .animation at L230 is nilled under Reduce Motion — so with no animation the insert/remove still occurs but instantly (no visible slide). Net effect under Reduce Motion is acceptable (instant swap), but the slide transition is declared unconditionally; cleaner to swap to .opacity-only under Reduce Motion. Verify there's no one-frame ghost. Curve/duration (easeInOut 0.35) is within the 250–400ms medium band but again a raw literal vs token. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L215-218, L230)`
- **Countdown progress bar uses .linear(0.1) keyed to promptProgress with a per-prompt .id reset — correct anti-rewind handling** — The fill animates .linear(0.1) (matching the 0.1s timer tick) and carries .id(viewModel.currentPromptIndex) so the per-window reset snaps to 0 instead of animating backwards across the wrap (comment L199-200). This is a thoughtful, correct content-gated reveal. Reduce Motion nils it (L198). Raw 0.1 literal is justified (ties to tick rate). No issue. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L196-201)`
- **Saved checkmark spring pop is Reduce-Motion-gated with a static fallback — correct** — onAppear: `guard !reduceMotion else { popped = true; return }` then withAnimation(.spring(response:0.5, dampingFraction:0.6)). Reduce Motion shows the checkmark already-popped (no scale/opacity animation). This matches DESIGN.md#L82 ('breathing collapses to a static glow'). Good. The spring is a raw literal but a one-off celebratory pop is a reasonable exception. `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L316-319)`
- **CrescentRing breathe/spin both gated; but the spec'd 'settles to a check when saved' transition is not implemented** — DESIGN.md#L80 and #L92 state the crescent 'settles to a check when saved.' In code the Saved state is a SEPARATE view (CheckInSavedView, CheckInView.swift#L75) with its own checkmark circle — there is no matchedGeometryEffect or morph from the recording ring into the saved check. The recording ring just disappears with the state crossfade and a brand-new checkmark pops in. The motion intent in DESIGN is unmet (the ring→check 'settle' is faked as a cut). Not broken, but a spec divergence and a missed signature moment. Breathe/spin gating itself is correct (CrescentRing.swift#L38,L45). `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CrescentRing.swift#L33-51; DESIGN.md#L80,L92)`
- **No haptic accompanies any motion in the flow** — Motion-without-haptic on the save pop and glyph selection. For this audience, pairing the checkmark spring with Haptics.success() and glyph taps with Haptics.selection() would reinforce the 'captured' moment. The enum exists (Haptics.swift) and is unused here. (Cross-listed in P1 findings.) `(app-four/Views/CheckIn/CheckInView.swift#L318 (pop), GlyphRampPicker.swift#L15 (selection))`

## 9. What might be missing

- No haptic feedback anywhere in the capture flow despite a ready Haptics enum — the single biggest missing affordance for the stated reward model (DESIGN.md#L86, Haptics.swift unused).
- No save-failure surface: stopRecording's catch silently resets to idle (CheckInViewModel.swift#L117) and saveTextCheckIn returns Void with no error channel — a lost capture is invisible to the user.
- No VoiceOver announcements for prompt rotation or state transitions, and no live-region timer — the recording experience is effectively eyes-only (CheckInView.swift#L190-246).
- The spec'd voice-level glow on the recording crescent is unimplemented — audioLevelStream is subscribed and discarded (CheckInViewModel.swift#L294); DESIGN.md#L80 calls for it.
- The spec'd crescent→check 'settle' morph is missing — Saved is a separate view with a fresh checkmark rather than the ring settling (DESIGN.md#L80,L92 vs CheckInView.swift#L75).
- No undo / 'oops, didn't mean to save' on the Saved screen — for an impulsive-capture audience, a brief undo would fit the 'forgiving, easy undo' principle (DESIGN.md#L71).
- No interruption handling visible at this layer — RecordingState has .paused and InterruptionType (phoneCall, etc.) exist (AppEnums.swift#L34), but CheckInView renders .paused identically to .recording (CheckInView.swift#L72) with no 'paused — resume?' affordance; unclear what a mid-recording phone call shows.
- Composer 'Save check-in' offers no confirmation and the sheet-dismiss→Saved-screen handoff is implicit; also no character/length guidance on the note (TextCheckInComposer.swift#L94).
- No empty/first-run differentiation on the hub — a brand-new user and a 100-day user see the identical 'Ready when you are.' with no one-time hint about what a check-in is or that voice is the fast path.
- Max-duration auto-stop (8 min, CheckInViewModel.swift#L282) gives no warning as it approaches — a user mid-thought is cut off and dropped to Saved with no 'time's up' notice; for time-blind users a gentle approach indicator would help.

## 10. Brainstorm seeds (→ /brainstorm)

1. The Saved screen ('Captured.') is currently a dead-end pair of identical buttons. What is the intended next move after a capture — settle and leave, immediately chain another, or glimpse what was just extracted? What does 'done' feel like for this user?
2. The recording prompts rotate on a timer regardless of whether the user has answered them. Should the nudges be a gentle ambient rhythm, a checklist the user can sense progress against, or fully ignorable scenery? What is their actual job?
3. Voice is the designed-for fast path, yet 'Type note' and 'Log meds' sit at equal visual weight below it. When does a user reach for typing over speaking, and should the hub adapt to that person's habit over time?
4. Haptics are entirely absent. What should the body feel at the moment of capture, on each glyph selection, and as a dose 'kicks in' — and where would haptic feedback cross from reassuring into nagging for this audience?
5. An interrupted or auto-stopped recording (phone call, 8-minute cap) currently drops silently. What is the kindest way to handle 'your recording got cut off' without it reading as failure or loss?
6. The crescent is described as breathing→spinning→settling-to-a-check, but Saved is a separate view today. Is the unbroken ring→check morph worth building as the product's signature motion, or is the calm cut actually better?
7. For a first-time user, the hub says 'Ready when you are.' with no orientation. What is the ideal first-run moment — does the app demonstrate a check-in, whisper one hint, or stay silent and trust discovery?
8. Nothing on this screen ever references a past check-in or a streak (by design). Is there a non-gamified way to make returning feel rewarding — a sense of continuity or 'the field remembers you' — without reintroducing the shame mechanics the product rejects?
