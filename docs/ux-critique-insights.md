# Insights — UX/UI Critique & Brainstorm Brief

> **Source:** opus max-effort code review (six lenses) **+ device screenshots**. Nielsen heuristics **31/40**.

> **Status:** uncommitted working doc — feeds superpowers `brainstorming` → (if a feature emerges) Spec Kit.


## 1. What it is

A 5-section vertically-paging Insights screen for a privacy-first ADHD check-in journal. Each "page" snaps to one viewport via `.scrollTargetBehavior(.paging)` (InsightsView.swift:101-114): (1) breakdown — an area-proportional MoodBubbleChart + dot legend; (2) signals — three horizontal "bead strips" (one per check-in day) for mood/energy/focus, plus a dashed "Sleep · not tracked yet" chip; (3) averages — three vertical fill gauges with dashed ticks; (4) rhythm — a 3-signal × 4-time-bucket dominant-level matrix; (5) connections — three correlation cards (med×focus, energy×mood, sleep×mood) gated behind a day-count threshold until "unlocked". A horizontal month selector rides at the top of page 1. Tapping a bead opens a DayDetailSheet; tapping a recording navigates to RecordingDetailView. All metrics are synchronous computed properties derived on-the-fly from `store.recordings` in an @Observable @MainActor view model.


## 2. Verdict

This is a genuinely thoughtful, non-slop screen — it passes the AI-slop test comfortably. The data viz is bespoke (area-proportional bubbles, ordinal bead strips, gauge ticks), the copy is warm and human ("today vs your usual", "3 or more days to unlock"), the empty/gated states are deliberate, and the design-system discipline is high (Theme tokens, Typography roles with relativeTo, no raw hex in views, color-is-never-the-only-cue honored via glyph shapes + labels). Product posture is on-spec: no streaks, no shame, gated cards explain exactly what to do. That said, there are real defects worth fixing before ship. The most serious is a paging architecture that hard-clips each section to one device viewport (page(height:) at InsightsView.swift:119-123) with no internal scroll — under large Dynamic Type or on small devices the rhythm matrix, the 280pt gauges, the bubble chart, and especially the connections cards will overflow and be silently truncated with no way to reach them. Second, the inactive-header dimming relies on `.scrollPosition(id:)` resolving SectionIDs nested inside the paging wrappers, which may never update — needs a render to confirm but the indirection is fragile and that dimming is the screen's primary position cue. Third, the month selector deliberately scrolls away (contradicting DESIGN.md's "Pinned selector"), so the month control is unreachable on 4 of 5 pages. Heuristics land at 31/40 — strong but with clear visibility and feedback gaps.


## 3. Strengths (preserve)

- Passes the AI-slop test: bespoke, legible data viz (area-proportional bubble chart with sqrt-area scaling at MoodBubbleChart.swift:31-36; ordinal bead strips; gauge tick lines) rather than a generic card grid. Copy is warm and specific ('today vs your usual' InsightsView.swift:69; 'Each bead is one check-in day, in order' :162).
- Design-system discipline is high: every color routes through Theme/Palette tokens, Typography roles all use relativeTo: for Dynamic Type (Typography.swift:13-42), and the one sanctioned black/white use (text-on-fill contrast) is centralized in Color.contrastingInk (SignalLevel.swift:12-18) with an explicit comment forbidding raw black/white elsewhere.
- Color-is-never-the-only-cue is genuinely honored: every bead/blob/gauge pairs the ramp color with a text label (displayLabel) and, for self-state signals, a distinct glyph shape (SignalGlyph). Absent data is a dashed outline, never a misleading level-1 glyph (SignalGlyph.swift:66-73; DailyRhythmMatrix.swift:61-71).
- Gated connection cards give explicit, actionable unlock copy ('Log medication on 2 more days…' InsightsViewModel+Signals.swift:193) instead of a dead 'not enough data', and pluralization is handled (dayNoun; 'check-in'/'check-ins'). On-spec ADHD posture: no streaks, no gamification.
- Accessibility scaffolding is above average: nearly every composite element sets accessibilityElement(children:.combine/.contain) with a synthesized label (bubble :78, bead :93-99, gauge :99-101, connection card :72/:107, rhythm cell :77-82), decorative glyphs are correctly hidden via decorative:true, and rhythm/bead hit targets are floored at 44pt (DailyRhythmMatrix.swift:73; SignalStripsView.swift:68).
- Empty and gated states are first-class: a dedicated emptyState (InsightsView.swift:216-229), a per-signal gauge '—' state (SignalAverageGauges.swift:49), a dashed sleep-deferred chip, and gated vs unlocked connection cards.

## 5. Screenshot layer (visual findings)

- **Confirms AI-slop PASS — hard.** Five distinct, editorial visualizations (Connections, Daily-rhythm matrix, "Where you averaged" gauges, bead strips, overlapping-circles breakdown). Nobody calls this generic.
- **Confirms the overflow P1 visually:** "Daily rhythm" and "Where you averaged" show huge empty bottom space (each section = one paged viewport) — under large Dynamic Type the content clips with no internal scroll.
- **Chart-metaphor integrity (breakdown, shot 9):** the 5 mood levels are drawn as **overlapping/Venn circles**, but it's a **part-to-whole distribution** (Low14/Flat32/Okay29/Good11/Great14). Overlap implies set-intersection that doesn't exist; also verify circle **area** encodes %.
- **Gating mechanic:** "3+ days to unlock", locked Sleep×Mood, "Sleep · not tracked yet". Sets expectations but withholds — UX question.
- **Top-edge ghosting + FAB overlap** present here too; verify dark-mode contrast on the olive energy bead/bar.

## 6. Accessibility audit

- **P1** Dynamic Type overflow: fixed-height gauges (280pt) and viewport-clipped pages truncate at large text sizes — Fonts scale via relativeTo (good), but the containers don't: gauges are a fixed 280pt, matrix blobs a fixed 52pt, and each section is clipped to one viewport (finding #1). At AX-sizes the captions, gauge labels, and section content grow but the page height does not, so text clips or content falls off the bottom with no scroll to reach it. This is the dominant a11y risk on the screen. `(app-four/Views/Insights/SignalAverageGauges.swift:24; app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:119-123)`
- **P2** VoiceOver has no announcement of which of the 5 pages is active; position is conveyed only by header opacity — Section identity is conveyed only by opacity (0.4 vs 1.0). Headers carry .isHeader (good for the rotor), but there is no announcement of which page is active when paging, and the dimmed-header text at 0.4 opacity on Theme.textPrimary may itself fall below contrast minimums while still being read by VoiceOver. Consider an accessibilityValue like 'page 2 of 5' and verify dimmed-header contrast. `(app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:127-129; app-four/Views/Insights/InsightsSectionHeader.swift:25-26)`
- **P2** Tap-to-open-day on beads has no hint and no visual affordance of tappability — Beads are Buttons (good) with an accessibilityLabel of date+level, but there's no accessibilityHint that tapping opens the day, and the .accessibilityLabel override can suppress default action phrasing depending on grouping. Sighted users also get no visual cue that a bead is interactive. Add a hint ('Opens this day') and a visual affordance. `(app-four/Views/Insights/SignalStripsView.swift:71-77)`
- **P2** Connection MiniBar drops its axis labels from the VoiceOver summary — UnlockedCard combines children and announces 'title: sentence barLabel' (:72), dropping leadingText/trailingText ('Med days' / 'Sharp+ focus'). Those labels carry what the % is OF, so VoiceOver users lose the axis framing. Fold leading/trailing into the combined label or expose them as accessibilityValue. `(app-four/Views/Insights/ConnectionCardsView.swift:71-72, :111-149)`
- **P3** Color-fill text contrast uses a 0.6 white-luminance threshold, not a WCAG ratio — contrastingInk picks black/white by getWhite > 0.6 — a heuristic, not a contrast-ratio computation. Mid-luminance ramp colors near the boundary (focus 'present' #5889BA, energy 'steady' #D2BB40) may land on the wrong side and yield <4.5:1 against the chosen ink for the small % / label text. Verify the actual ratio of each ramp level against the ink it selects. `(app-four/DesignSystem/SignalLevel.swift:12-18)`
- **P3** Empty-data dashed outlines may fail non-text contrast and are visually faint — Empty cells/beads use Theme.textSecondary.opacity(0.3–0.4) dashed strokes plus an em-dash. A dash at 30% opacity likely fails 3:1 non-text contrast on the paper background, making 'no data' hard to perceive for low-vision users. VoiceOver gets 'no data' (good), but the visual cue is weak. `(app-four/Views/Insights/DailyRhythmMatrix.swift:61-71; app-four/Views/Insights/SignalStripsView.swift:86-90)`

## 7. Logic / correctness

- **P2** Rhythm tie-break always rounds dominance up, biasing the displayed level toward the high end — On a frequency tie, topValue = freq.filter{maxCount}.keys.max() picks the highest tied level. A bucket with equal 'foggy' and 'sharp' shows 'Sharp'. Systematic upward bias misrepresents the user's actual rhythm; define tie-breaking intentionally (closest-to-mean, or lower-on-tie) and document it. `(app-four/ViewModels/InsightsViewModel+Signals.swift:172-174)`
- **P3** Latent infinite loop in availableMonths on date-arithmetic nil — date = calendar.date(byAdding:.month, value:1, to:date) ?? end with loop bound date <= end and no break: a nil result pins date to end and re-appends end forever. Not reachable with Gregorian valid month-starts, but it's an unguarded loop whose Optional fallback points back into its own bound. Use a bounded for-loop or break when the next date fails to strictly advance. `(app-four/ViewModels/InsightsViewModel.swift:41-46)`
- **P3** signalStrips recomputes monthRecordings inside nested per-day loops (O(days × recordings)) — days is built from monthRecordings, then for each day it re-filters monthRecordings again (:115). monthRecordings is itself a full-store filter (InsightsViewModel.swift:49-53) with no caching, so the cost compounds across the screen's many computed metrics on every body evaluation. Hoist monthRecordings into a local and group by day once. `(app-four/ViewModels/InsightsViewModel+Signals.swift:111-124)`
- **P3** prevMonth / nextMonth / jumpToToday on the view model are never called by this view (dead surface) — The selector sets currentMonth directly via binding; prevMonth/nextMonth/jumpToToday are unused by InsightsView. Either wire them (e.g. a 'jump to today' affordance — see Missing) or remove them (project rule: no dead code). `(app-four/ViewModels/InsightsViewModel.swift:59-69; app-four/Views/Insights/MonthSelectorScrollView.swift:20)`
- **P3** selectedDay sheet path can present a CalendarDay whose recordings array is recomputed but day identity is the Date — stale-data risk on month change — calendarDay(for:) builds a CalendarDay snapshot of recordings at tap time; the sheet binds to viewModel.selectedDay (id = date). If the store mutates while the sheet is open, the sheet holds the old snapshot (CalendarDay captures a recordings array, not a live query). Low impact for a journal but worth noting that the sheet won't reflect a new check-in added to that day while open. `(app-four/ViewModels/InsightsViewModel.swift:9-17, :76-85; app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:44-46)`

## 8. Motion / transitions

- **Header dim animation is correctly Reduce-Motion-gated, but the value it animates may never change** — headerAnimation returns nil under reduceMotion (good, honors DESIGN.md:82) and otherwise Motion.snappy; each section applies .animation(headerAnimation, value: activeSectionID). The gating is correct; the risk (finding #2) is that activeSectionID may not advance because the .id() targets are nested inside the paging wrappers, so the animation is well-formed but possibly never triggered. `(app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:131-133, :155/:173/:185/:199/:213)`
- **Tab-reselect reset uses a raw easeOut literal, not a Motion token, and isn't Reduce-Motion-gated** — onChange(of: selectedTab) wraps the activeSectionID reset in withAnimation(.easeOut(duration: 0.25)). Per DESIGN.md motion should route through Motion tokens (no magic numbers); 0.25 easeOut is a raw literal and always animates regardless of Reduce Motion. Use Motion.snappy and gate on reduceMotion. Same raw-literal pattern in ScreenContainer.swift:70. `(app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:48-52)`
- **Month selector underline/weight transition is not Reduce-Motion-gated** — .animation(Motion.snappy, value: currentMonth) animates the selected underline and bold weight. It uses the token (good) but is unconditional; under Reduce Motion the underline slide and weight change should be instant. Wrap with a reduceMotion-aware nil like headerAnimation does. `(app-four/Views/Insights/MonthSelectorScrollView.swift:34)`
- **No transition on empty→populated swap; the layout switches abruptly** — The Group switches between sectionsScroll and the empty VStack on viewModel.hasAnyData with no transition. When the first check-in of a month lands, the screen pops from empty-state to full paging instantly. A gentle settle (DESIGN.md:81) would fit the 'always slightly calmer than you' posture; acceptable as-is but noted. `(app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:24-36)`
- **Paging snap has no explicit Reduce-Motion fallback; .paging is a system behavior so likely honored, but unverified** — .scrollTargetBehavior(.paging) drives the core section-to-section motion. System scroll behaviors generally respect Reduce Motion, but the whole interaction model is the snap; confirm on-device with Reduce Motion that paging still works and isn't jarring (or provide a non-paging fallback, which would also fix finding #1). `(app-four/Views/InsightsView.swift:111)`

## 9. What might be missing

- No 'jump to current month / today' affordance surfaced in the UI even though jumpToToday() exists (InsightsViewModel.swift:68): after paging back through months a user must scroll the selector manually to return to now.
- No per-section accessibility status ('page 2 of 5') or page-indicator dots — the only cue to position in the 5-section stack is header opacity, which is visual-only and possibly non-functional (finding #2).
- No error state anywhere — only empty vs populated. If recordings fail to load there is no messaging (InsightsView.swift:24-36).
- No loading state despite all metrics computed synchronously over the full store on the main actor.
- No way to act on an insight from the Insights surface: beads navigate to a day, but the connection cards — the most 'insightful' element — are inert (tapping reveals nothing).
- Sleep is shown as 'not tracked yet' yet drives a connection card and has seed data — a missing/contradictory tracking surface for sleep.
- No empty-strip framing per signal: if a month has mood data but no energy data, the energy strip renders all-dashed beads with summary 'no data' (+Signals.swift:313) but no higher-level 'energy wasn't logged this month' message.
- No date range / multi-month or all-time view — Insights is hard-scoped to one calendar month, truncating longitudinal pattern-finding that an ADHD self-tracking use case benefits from.
- No share/export of insights, and no on-screen reminder that this is on-device (the app's core privacy promise).
- The screen promises 'today vs your usual' (InsightsView.swift:69) but nothing on the page actually contrasts today against the month aggregate — the framing is currently unmet.

## 10. Brainstorm seeds (→ /brainstorm)

1. For an ADHD audience prone to overwhelm, is a 5-section forced-paging carousel the right container, or does snapping/hiding content (and losing the month control on 4/5 pages) add navigation load that a single calm scroll would avoid?
2. What is the intended emotional takeaway of Insights — reassurance ('you showed up'), self-knowledge ('here's your pattern'), or actionability ('do more of X')? The current mix (bubbles + gated correlations + rhythm) spans all three without a clear primary.
3. Should connection cards be interactive — tapping to reveal the specific days/check-ins behind '75% of the time' — to make the correlations trustworthy and explorable rather than asserted?
4. How should sleep be represented before its ramp ships: hidden entirely, a single bed-icon strip, or surfaced only in connections — and how do we keep that consistent so 'not tracked yet' never contradicts a sleep insight?
5. What time window should Insights cover — strict calendar month, rolling 30 days, or selectable ranges including all-time — given that ADHD pattern-finding benefits from longer horizons than one month?
6. How do we deliver on 'today vs your usual' (the screen's own framing) — is there a 'today' marker or comparison anywhere, or should that promise be dropped until it's built?
7. What's the right correlation-confidence and tie-breaking story (current code biases dominance upward and counts any-qualifying-day), so the app never overstates a pattern to a population sensitive to false self-narratives?
8. Should Insights degrade gracefully into a 'come back after a few check-ins' nurture state with partial reveals, rather than a single binary empty-state, to reward early, sporadic logging without implying failure?
