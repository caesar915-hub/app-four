1

Screen frame & medication bar

One continuous scroll — the 5-page snap pager was retired 2026-07-16 (a07, spec 036). Content fades into the tab bar (bottom edge-fade 36).

The medication capsule floats under the nav bar on every screen. Passive by design: progress only ever goes forward, a worn-off dose just goes quiet — no red alarms (locked anti-reference).

2

Identity + presence line (proposed)

The screen frames every number as you against your own baseline — never against norms, streaks, or other people.

Proposed: a presence line where other apps put a streak. It counts the days you showed up without ever counting the miss — Daylio's entry streak, de-shamed (their 10,000-day badge and sad-day achievements are exactly what PRODUCT.md forbids). The “mostly Good” tail answers “and how was it?” in the same breath.

3

Month scope chips

One chip per month since the first check-in; switching month re-scopes every card on the screen. Shown scrolled to the selected (current) month.

New Look chip grammar: selected = solid meadow fill + white label; unselected = white + hairline + ink.

4

Overall check-in breakdown

Mood distribution as area-proportional bubbles — area, not radius, carries the share, so a dominant mood reads honestly instead of exaggerating.

Position is semantic: valence low→great left→right, with a slight vertical float for higher moods. Percent inside the bubble, raw counts in the legend — colour is never the only cue (principle 5).

5

Month in three signals — weekday strips

The month's weekly shape at a glance: each bead is the average level for that weekday, drawn as the signal's own glyph (sprout / bolt / aperture).

Level is encoded by the glyph's shape ladder — size, fill, notches, rings — so it survives grayscale and colour-blindness. A genuinely empty weekday would be a dashed outline, never a fake level-1 glyph (FR-015).

Sleep is spec'd but its ramp is deferred, so it shows as an honest dashed “not tracked yet” chip.

Proposed takeaway line: the strips hold the answer but make the user find it; one plain sentence names it. Daylio ships the same data as premium weekday bar charts — Squirl already has the better visual, so the upgrade is language, not another chart.

6

Where you averaged — gauges

One vertical gauge per signal, filling bottom-up to the month's mean on the 1–5 ordinal scale. Five dashed ticks mark the ordinal steps.

The wording avoids false precision: a fractional mean reads “Okay+” with the caption “between Okay & Good”, never a decimal.

7

This week vs your usual (proposed)

Bearable's weekly report is its most-loved surface — the last 7 days set against the 7 before. Squirl adapts it inline: three rows, no digest, no push notification.

It finally delivers the subtitle's “today vs your usual” promise with an actual contrast. The wording tiers do the emotional work: small changes read “about the same”, and the worst it can ever say is “down a little”.

8

Your daily rhythm

Signal × time-of-day matrix. Each cell is the dominant (most frequent) level in that bucket — the mode, not the mean — so one unusually loud check-in can't swing a cell.

Ties break toward the higher level; a bucket with no check-ins renders as a dashed outline with an em-dash — honest emptiness, not fog.

9

Connections v2 (proposed)

Co-occurrence (“56% of med days had sharp focus”) upgrades to a contrast — the with-vs-without difference that is Daylio's and Bearable's crown jewel, paywalled in both. Squirl's version stays deterministic: two means and a documented denominator.

The High confidence chip and the plain sample line do what neither competitor manages — Daylio's confidence is an opaque model, Bearable shows none at all. Tiers are fixed and shown in-app.

Card 2 stays on the current co-occurrence generation for comparison; card 3 previews the 1-day-lag logic while gated. The eyebrow copy fixes the “3 or more days” mismatch (gates are 4 / 5 / 3+3) called out in the UX critique.

10

Tab bar

Four tabs; Insights is chart.bar.fill. Selection tint is meadow green; unselected sit at secondary ink. Scroll content dissolves into the bar via the bottom edge-fade.
