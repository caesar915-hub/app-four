The calendar, decided

Every decision from the exploration, with the final design it produced: the screen, the day card, the check-in, and the signal language behind them.

THE SCREEN

Medication bar, calendar, then the days

Fixed order: the medication bar is the first component, the calendar day-row sits below it, the day-card list below that. Selecting a date filters out the days above it, so the chosen day jumps to the top, already expanded; older days stay below. Only the med bar is pinned.

Why this shape
Med bar firstIt’s the one always-relevant control; pinned so it never scrolls away.
Calendar below itWeek row with mood + medication dots. No ring around today,today is shown only by the “Today” pill.
Filter-aboveTap a date → days more recent than it are filtered out (greyed in the row); the day jumps to top and auto-expands. Scroll down for older days.
No accent borderThe selected day carries no gold/amber border,position + expansion are enough.
THE DAY CARD

A Meadow block that opens to the day

Folded, the card is a soft Meadow block (the day's mood tint) that reads the whole day at a glance: the mood glyph sits in a cream badge, the mood word + weekday on one line, a divider, then energy · focus · medication (name only). Opening keeps the Meadow strip as the header and drops the check-in rows onto cream below. Tap either card to fold / unfold.

Folded
Meadow block · cream badge = mood glyph · title: mood word · weekday · divider · then energy · focus · medication name. Tap to open.
Unfolded
The Meadow strip stays as the header (cream badge + mood word + weekday); check-in rows drop onto cream below — time inline beside the mood, details chevron on the trailing edge. Tap to fold.
THE CHECK-IN

Time-circle, phase ring, signals

Each check-in is a circle holding the mood glyph, wrapped in the purple medication-phase ring with its % below. To the right: the mood word with the time inline beside it, then energy + focus as ramp glyphs that encode their own level, then chips for medication, feelings and side-effects. A details chevron on the trailing edge opens the full entry.

Decisions
Circle = medication %The ring shows dose phase; the % sits under it. The circle holds the mood glyph; time is a label inline by the mood word, never inside the ring.
Signals = glyphsMood word leads; energy + focus shown as ramp glyphs (shape + hue + fill carry the level). Partial check-ins show only what was logged.
ChipsMedication (name), feelings, side-effects. Light pills below the signals.
Details affordanceA chevron on the trailing edge opens the full check-in; time moved inline next to the mood word to free that edge.
SIGNAL LANGUAGE

Four signals, one glyph system

Each signal is a distinct shape + hue + fill, legible by form alone (colour is never the only cue). Mood, energy and focus climb 1→5; medication is a single capsule, shown by name and dose.

DESIGN TOKENS

Paper & Pollen

Warm paper, never pure white or black. Fraunces for display and big numbers, DM Sans for UI, IBM Plex Mono for data. Calm, unhurried, made for ADHD: summary first, detail on tap, nothing ever shaming a quiet day.

PRINCIPLES & INTERACTION

How it behaves

Summary firstFolded cards carry the read; detail appears only on tap.
Shrink on openThe folded summary collapses as the rows open, so detail isn’t competing with a tall header.
No shameA quiet day reads as permission (“that’s alright”), never red or overdue. No streaks, no scores.
Time made visibleMedication-phase ring + timestamps externalise time, built for time-blindness.
Redundant encodingEvery signal = shape + hue + fill, so it survives colour-blindness and greyscale.
PredictableLayout never reshuffles; the calendar is always under the med bar.
★ The references that set the bar: Tiimo (App of the Year, calm, anti-shame, time-visible), Reflectly (beautiful journaling), Fantastical / Amie (calendar craft), Daylio (seconds to log), Bearable / Structured (legible timelines).
